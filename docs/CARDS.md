# Recording cards

Twenty-five scenarios. You are a distribution rep standing in a shop, telling head
office what you just saw.

### How to use a card

1. Read the card. Understand the situation.
2. Put the phone away from your face — a normal speaking distance, not a podcast mic.
3. **Say it in your own words.** The Bangla line under each card is a suggestion, not a
   script. If you read it out flatly, the recording is useless: read speech and spoken
   speech sound different to a recogniser, and the whole point is to test real speech.
4. Keep it under about twenty seconds.
5. Say the shop name naturally, the way a rep would.

### The numbers are the point

Where a card says **দেড় ডজন**, say দেড় ডজন — not "eighteen". The Bangla quantity grammar
is the part of the system being tested, and saying the resolved number instead bypasses
exactly the thing we are measuring.

### If you get it wrong

Say it again in the same recording, or record a second take. Both are fine and neither
needs a note. Only write in the `notes` column if you said something **different from the
card** — a different shop, a different product, a different number. That changes the
answer key and we need to know.

## Multi-observation cards

Two or three separate things in one breath. **Do not pause between them** — say it the way
a rep actually would, as one continuous report. Splitting a clip into separate sentences
makes the segmentation trivial and hides what we are testing.

### Card 16 · Bijoy Store — 2 things
Bijoy Store wants one and a half dozen PRAN mango juice, and Wheel has a new offer at five
taka cheaper.

> বিজয় স্টোরে প্রাণ ম্যাঙ্গো জুস দেড় ডজন লাগবে, আর হুইল এর নতুন অফার দিছে পাঁচ টাকা কম

## Recording plan — 40 clips from 25 cards

Record **all 25 cards once**, then pick **15 cards and record them again** with a different
speaker or in a different noise level. That gives 40 clips and means some scenarios appear
twice under different conditions — which is what lets us chart accuracy against noise.

Name each file `clip-<card>-<take>`:

```
clip-01-a     card 1, first take
clip-01-b     card 1, second take (different speaker or noise)
clip-16-a     card 16
```

### The mix that matters

| | target |
|---|---|
| quiet — indoors, no traffic | ~13 clips |
| moderate — street, some background | ~13 clips |
| loud — market, traffic, people talking over you | ~14 clips |
| distinct speakers | at least 3, ideally 4 |

**Loud clips are not a compromise, they are the experiment.** The entire claim this project
makes is that the transcript comes out badly and the extracted fields come out right anyway.
If every clip is quiet, the transcript comes out fine and we have proved nothing.

### Dhaka-standard Bangla only

The evaluation silently discards any clip labelled with another dialect — no warning, no
count. If a speaker has a strong Chittagong or Sylhet accent, that clip cannot be used, so
don't spend one on them.
